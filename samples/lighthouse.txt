The lighthouse keeper's log

Monday. Fog from the east before dawn. Lit the lamp early and kept it
turning until the sun burned through at ten. Three fishing boats passed
the point, all of them slow, all of them close to the rocks. Counted the
gulls on the rail: eleven. Oiled the gears. Tea, bread, and the same
sea as yesterday.

Tuesday. Clear sky, steady wind. The supply boat came at noon with
flour, lamp oil, and a sack of letters, most of them for the last
keeper. Mended the net that holds the weather vane. The sea was green
near the shore and grey farther out. Counted the gulls on the rail:
nine. Two were missing, or two were new.

Wednesday. Rain all day. Nothing passed the point. Read the tide tables
from cover to cover and found one error, on page sixteen, where the
evening tide is listed before the morning tide. Wrote it in the margin.
Tea, bread, and the same sea as yesterday, only wetter.

Thursday. Fog again. The lamp, the gears, the stairs: one hundred and
twelve steps up, one hundred and twelve steps down, and somehow more
on the way up. A whale surfaced near the eastern rocks and stayed for
an hour. Counted the gulls on the rail: eleven again.

Friday. Wind from the north, strong enough to hum in the rails. A
schooner sheltered behind the point until the evening. Signalled them
the tide and they signalled back their thanks. Cleaned the lens until
it showed the whole room upside down.

Saturday. Calm. The sea as flat as a table. Painted the door the same
red as before, since red is the only paint there is. The supply boat
is not due until Tuesday, and the tea will last until Wednesday, so
all is well.

Sunday. Rest, mostly. Lit the lamp at dusk as on every other day,
since the rocks do not rest. Counted the gulls on the rail: eleven.
The same sea as yesterday, and the same as tomorrow, and never once
the same.
